Mode & system

Mode:

Baseline stays locked until every reach in the view has a sourced (MEASURED / DEPARTMENT) head. Until then, everything on this page is a scenario. Compare mode adds a second turbine column for the selected reach — the right way to settle a turbine choice, by annual energy, not by peak kW.

System:

Do not add the reaches up. Branches and minors are off-takes of the main canal, so the same water appears in several reaches. Any system total below is an upper-bound screening figure, not additive generation. In a real cascade each fall can only use the drop at its own structure, once.

Scenario controls — what you are assuming

These controls are the assumptions. Change one and every reach, total and rank updates. Nothing here is a measurement.

Head — gross to net

One scenario head is applied to the selected reach at a time, exactly as in v1. It is not a measured head for that fall. Per-reach sourced heads replace this slider, reach by reach, when they arrive.

Total losses: 0.22 m  ·  Net head: 1.78 m

Worked example (ILLUSTRATIVE, from the practical guide):
Gross 2.50 m − losses 0.50 m (rack 0.25 + intake 0.10 + friction 0.04 + exit 0.11) = net 2.00 m.
At Q = 4.00 m³/s, η = 0.85: gross 9.81 × 4.00 × 2.50 × 0.85 = 83 kW → net 9.81 × 4.00 × 2.00 × 0.85 = 67 kW.
Half a metre of losses is a 20% correction at low head. Any twin that skips losses overstates every reach.

Flow

Season presets set the flow slider. The monthly table below then shapes that flow across the year. Closure (0%) gives exactly 0 kW — a month at zero is honest; a fantasy few kW is not.

Turbine — per selected reach

Auto-suggestion: head first, flow second, flow variability third — then price and local service. It runs when you press the button, and whenever head or flow changes.

Two units: in low season one unit runs near its best point while the other rests. The twin models that — if monthly flow is at or below half of design, only one unit runs, at twice the per-unit ratio.

Turbine library — 8 families

Head first (it eliminates most options), flow second (it sizes the machine), flow variability third (fixed vs adjustable, one unit vs two), and only then price and local service. Ranges are typical screening values — they shortlist options; they never replace a supplier's guaranteed curve.

Data honesty — what is real, what is scenario

Real — from your uploaded tables

  • Design discharge for all 21 reaches, in cusecs, from your progress-report tables (Lower Swat and Kabul River). Converted at 1 cusec = 0.0283168 m³/s.
  • Length and CCA per reach, and each reach's off-take / chainage note, as in those tables. Kabul River Main Canal has no CCA in the table — shown as “—”, not invented.
  • Group, supervisor, and the 6 Nov 2026 presentation date.

Scenario — assumed, labelled on screen

  • Head: the gross-head slider is an assumption (ASSUMED badge). No sourced head exists for any fall yet.
  • Seasonal factors: planning factors, ESTIMATED. Not the department release schedule.
  • Turbine curves: typical shapes, not a supplier's guaranteed curve for a named machine.
  • Costs: ILLUSTRATIVE planning numbers, never a quotation.

The 3 datasets that would make it real

  1. Sourced heads per fall — upstream and downstream levels, date, season, who measured or supplied them.
  2. Structure list with chainage — every fall, regulator and off-take, so a reach on screen is a place a person can stand.
  3. Release / closure schedule — when the canal runs, at what discharge, and when it closes for desilting or rotation.

If the departments do not have the data, the alternatives (per the post-presentation plan, 7 Nov–5 Dec 2026) are: measure head yourselves (staff gauge / levelling) and flow yourselves (float method, ×0.85, Q = A × v), build the structure list by GPS field survey, and build the schedule from gate staff and farmers. A DEM must not be used as a head measurement. Requested is not received — keep the receipt log until a file is in your hands.
